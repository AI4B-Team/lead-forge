Billing WebhooksStripe
Stripe integration

How the billing sync works when your billing platform is Stripe: you point your Stripe account's webhooks at our webhook service and tag your Stripe prices with a small amount of metadata; we take care of the rest.

How it works​

We listen to four Stripe events from your account and translate them into account operations on your white-label platform:

Stripe event	What we do
customer.subscription.created	Create the user's platform account (if it doesn't already exist).
customer.subscription.updated	Reconcile: activate, change plan, suspend (on past_due/unpaid), or reactivate to match the subscription.
customer.subscription.deleted	Deactivate the platform account. This is permanent — deactivation removes the user's data.
checkout.session.completed	If the purchased price is tagged as an addon, grant the addon (feature access or credits) to the user's account.

Only prices tagged with our metadata are acted on. Anything untagged is silently ignored, so you can safely route your entire Stripe account at our endpoint — unrelated products and subscriptions are skipped.

Two ways to onboard​
Option A — invite us to your Stripe account (recommended)​

Invite Realeflow to your Stripe account as a team member with the Developer role (Stripe Dashboard → Settings → Team → + New member). Ask your Realeflow contact for the email address to invite.

With Developer access we set everything up for you: the webhook endpoints, the price metadata (matched to your plan configuration on our side), and the restricted API key. You review, we test together in test mode, done.

Option B — self-service setup​

If you'd rather not grant access, follow the checklist below and send us the resulting secrets through a secure channel (not plain email — use your Realeflow contact's preferred secret-sharing method).

Self-service setup checklist​
1. Create two webhook endpoints​

In Stripe Dashboard → Developers → Webhooks, create one endpoint per Stripe mode:

Stripe mode	Endpoint URL
Live	https://partner-api-webhooks.realeflow.com/stripe/<your-partner-slug>
Test	https://partner-api-webhooks-test.realeflow.com/stripe/<your-partner-slug>

Your Realeflow contact will give you your partner slug.

Subscribe each endpoint to exactly these four events:

customer.subscription.created
customer.subscription.updated
customer.subscription.deleted
checkout.session.completed

Careful: customer.subscription.paused sits right next to customer.subscription.updated in Stripe's event picker. Selecting paused by mistake makes account creation appear stuck, because we never see the updated event that activates the account.

2. Tag your subscription prices​

On every Stripe price that represents a platform subscription, add metadata:

Key	Required	Value
RFPlanId	yes	The Realeflow plan ID this price maps to. We provide these during onboarding.
RFTrialPlanId	optional	Plan ID to use while the subscription is trialing. Falls back to RFPlanId.
3. Tag your addon prices​

On every price sold through a one-time checkout / Payment Link that should grant something on the platform:

Key	Required	Value
RFAddOnProduct	yes	The addon code — we provide the exact values for your product lineup during onboarding (e.g. ai_county, pca_nationwide, liens).
RFAddOnQuantity	for credit-based addons	Integer credit count. Not needed for nationwide/unlimited or feature-toggle addons.
4. Send us the secrets​

For each mode (live and test), send your Realeflow contact:

The webhook signing secret (whsec_…) — shown on the endpoint's detail page in the Stripe dashboard.
A restricted API key with only Customers: Read permission (Developers → API keys → Create restricted key). We use it to read the customer's email and name when creating their platform account, and to match one-time addon purchases back to the right user.

We'll confirm once both environments are configured on our side.

5. (Recommended) Pass a durable ID on addon Payment Links​

Stripe Payment Links create a new Stripe customer on every checkout, so a one-time addon purchase doesn't automatically identify which of your users bought it. When your app links a logged-in user to an addon Payment Link, append two parameters:

https://buy.stripe.com/<id>?client_reference_id={their_stripe_customer_id}&prefilled_email={their_email}

client_reference_id — the user's Stripe customer ID from their subscription (or whatever identifier was used when their account was created). This lets us match the purchase to the account directly.
prefilled_email — pre-fills the checkout email field, cutting down typo mismatches.

Without these, the addon flow still works — we fall back to matching by email — but it's slower and fails if the buyer types an email Stripe has never seen.

Testing your integration​

Before going live, run through this in test mode:

Subscribe with test card 4242 4242 4242 4242 → the platform account should be created and active within a few seconds.
Change the subscription to a different tagged price → the platform plan should change.
Use a failing card or mark the subscription past_due → the account should suspend; fixing payment should reactivate it.
Buy each addon through its test Payment Link → credits/features should appear on the account.
Cancel the subscription → the account should deactivate.

Your Realeflow contact can watch our side of each event live during the test run.

Troubleshooting​
Stripe shows the webhook returning 401 — the signing secret we have doesn't match your endpoint. Each Stripe endpoint has its own secret; re-send the one from the endpoint's detail page.
Subscription created but no platform account — check the price has RFPlanId metadata, and that the endpoint is subscribed to all four events (see the paused vs updated pitfall above).
Addon purchased but not granted — check RFAddOnProduct metadata on the price, and that the buyer used the email they subscribed with (or that the link included client_reference_id).
Subscription stuck incomplete in test mode — declined or incomplete-3DS test cards never activate. Use 4242 4242 4242 4242.

Anything else: contact your Realeflow representative — failures on our side alert us in real time, so we often see the issue before you do.
